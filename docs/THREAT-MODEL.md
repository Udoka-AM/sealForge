# sealForge threat model

What sealForge protects, against whom, and where the limits are. Every row points at the code or test that enforces it. Where a protection isn't built, this document says so.

**Status (2026-11-21, `main` at `4b80b7d`):** auction logic, escrow cryptography (FROST 2-of-3 DKG and rerandomized signing of Ironwood spends through PCZTs), close and publish, receipts, both bid modes (deposit and sealed-reveal), refund planning, and the independent chain audit (CLI and in-browser) are built, tested, and have run on public Zcash testnet (auctions `KE5DFF4PDA` and `K7GHHBC4GP`, see [evidence/testnet-genesis.md](evidence/testnet-genesis.md) and [evidence/test-run.md](evidence/test-run.md)). Since then, trustee-side transaction and refund checks, signer agreement on commitments, trustee backups and long-term trustee keys are built and live on the testnet site, verified on a local regtest chain ([evidence/regtest-e2e.md](evidence/regtest-e2e.md)). No auction has run on mainnet. ShieldRamp's quote and swap leg is specified, not built.

## Trust assumptions

Stated first because every protection below depends on them.

1. **Deposit mode: the coordinator sees bid amounts live.** The coordinator holds the escrow UFVK to scan for bids, so it decrypts each bid's amount and memo as it arrives, and so does whoever operates it. Since the F10 fix, **trustees can too**: to check the escrow address they receive `nk ‖ rivk`, which with `ak` gives the incoming viewing key (trust assumption 2). A trustee working with one bidder could tell them rival bids before close. Checking the address and reading its notes can't be separated without a zero-knowledge proof. Sealed-reveal mode is the mitigation: it removes the price from what the coordinator and trustees see until the reveal window (see below).
2. **The coordinator samples `nk` and `rivk` and holds the escrow UFVK.** When the last trustee completes the DKG, the coordinator builds the Orchard FVK `ak ‖ nk ‖ rivk` from the FROST group key `ak` and fresh `nk`, `rivk` from `OsRng` (`crates/sealforge-coordinator/src/routes/dkg.rs`, `sealforge_escrow::address::escrow_fvk`). It stores the UFVK encrypted with `SF_DATA_KEY`. This gives the coordinator viewing power over the escrow, not spending power: spends need a RedPallas signature under `ak`, which needs two FROST shares.
   - The coordinator also seals `nk ‖ rivk` to each trustee. Each trustee rebuilds the UA from its own copy of `ak` and signs an attestation over it before the auction can open (`verify-escrow`). The cost: **trustees can view the escrow too** (incoming bids live, in deposit mode, and outgoing refunds), the same viewing power the coordinator has. Once it has checked the address, each trustee keeps the resulting viewing key (`escrow-view.json`, owner-only) and puts it in its backup string, so it holds this viewing power for good, not only while signing (see the next point). Sealed-reveal mode still hides prices until the reveal window.
   - A database dump **plus** `SF_DATA_KEY`: the attacker reads every escrow's incoming and outgoing notes, including bids before close (sealing breaks for deposit-mode amounts) and refund addresses. Funds don't move.
   - Two trustee machines, or two trustees' backup strings: the attacker can spend the escrow. Each trustee keeps the escrow FVK next to its key share (`escrow-view.json`, kept once it checked the FVK against the address it attested) and in its backup string, so two of them hold everything a spend needs, without the coordinator. That is also what lets two honest trustees recover the funds if the coordinator and its database are lost. One trustee's machine or backup alone views the escrow but can't spend.
3. **Trustees check what the coordinator builds.** Trustees now receive the PCZT and run the shared frost-zec co-signer checks (`sealforge-verify`) plus sealForge's batch rules before committing and before signing: they compute the sighash themselves, check every spend is the escrow's and every randomizer matches, decrypt every output with the escrow's full viewing key (rebuilt from their own key share and tied to the address they attested), refuse hidden outputs, foreign pools, excess fee, padding and far expiries, and allow only: a consolidation that pays nobody, a payout of one output to the payout address (or, when the auction charges a protocol fee, the payout plus one fee output of exactly the fee the published result implies, to the fee address the auction recorded at creation; a second payout that pays the fee again is refused through the same ledger), refunds of standard-size notes within what the published result owes. For refunds, trustees also read every claimed bid from the chain through a lightwallet server they chose and require each note to go to its bid's memo refund address and fit its refund split together with everything they signed before (a durable ledger; with 2-of-3 any two signing pairs share a trustee, so no note is paid twice). The browser trustee page runs the same checks (#54), through a lightwallet server the trustee sets on the page, and keeps its ledger in the browser's IndexedDB. That ledger lives only in that browser: clearing site data or switching between the browser and the CLI mid-auction starts a new one. A note can be paid twice only if both trustees of a signing pair lose their ledgers, since any two pairs share a trustee. Residual: trustees rely on the published result's refund amounts (which anyone can audit from the chain) and on the lightwallet server they chose.
4. **Liveness: organiser, coordinator, trustees.** Refunds start only after the organiser publishes (`POST /v1/auctions/{code}/publish` is organiser-only; nothing publishes automatically). Then they need the coordinator (to build PCZTs and aggregate) plus two of the three trustees. Any of them can delay an auction. Funds stay in the escrow until two trustees sign ([evidence/testnet-genesis.md](evidence/testnet-genesis.md) §Timing records a real coordinator outage).
5. **The published UIVK is permanent.** At publish, the UIVK is released so anyone can audit. From then on, anyone with it can decrypt every incoming note to that escrow: each deposit's amount and memo (auction code, commitment, refund address) and, in sealed-reveal mode, each reveal (price and salt). This is by design. It does not reveal who sent the payments, and it does not decrypt outgoing refunds or the payout.

## Assets

| Asset | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Bidder identity (which wallet bid) | The core promise: price public, bidders private |
| Bid amounts during the auction | Leaking them lets others bid strategically |
| Escrowed funds | Bidder money until refunds and payout |
| Integrity and completeness of the result | Price, winners and refunds must be checkable by anyone, including that no bid was left out |
| Refund flow | Refunds must not re-identify bidders |

## Adversaries and protections

| Adversary | Protected? | How | Enforced by |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Public chain observer, during the auction** | ✅ | Bids are shielded Ironwood payments. Amount, sender and memo are encrypted on chain | Zcash note encryption. Escrow UA has only an Orchard receiver (`sealforge_escrow::address::escrow_address`) |
| **Public chain observer, after publish** | ⚠️ Amounts revealed, ✅ senders hidden | The published UIVK decrypts incoming notes (amount, memo) but not senders. Refund addresses in memos become readable; the bid form asks for a fresh address per bid | `routes/auctions.rs` publish derives the UIVK from the encrypted UFVK. Trust assumption 5 |
| **Organiser or coordinator publishing a false result** | ✅ | Anyone recomputes price, volume, winners, every outcome and the Merkle root from the chain. Bidders check their own inclusion from their receipt | `sealforge_auditor::audit_chain`, `verify_receipt`. Tests: `inflated_volume_or_hidden_bid_is_caught`, `rewritten_outcome_is_caught`, `tampered_root_breaks_inclusion`. Testnet: [evidence/audit-rescan.md](evidence/audit-rescan.md) tamper test |
| **Organiser or coordinator hiding a bid from the published list** | ✅ within the published scan range | The auditor rescans the chain with the UIVK and a lightwallet server of the auditor's choice, reclassifies every payment with the same `sealforge-core::selection` code, and compares. A missing bid changes the bid count, outcomes and root. Residual: the default scan range is the published `scan_from_height..scan_to_height`; an auditor can widen it with `--from/--to` | `sealforge_chain::audit::scan_incoming`, `sealforge-scan`, `sealforge-audit` CLI, browser rescan. Commit-reveal: `commit_reveal_tests::a_suppressed_reveal_is_caught` |
| **Coordinator operator, deposit mode** | ⚠️ Amounts visible | It sees bid amounts live. The auction page says so | Trust assumption 1 |
| **Coordinator building a malicious refund or payout** | ✅ (pending the external audit) | Trustees decode the PCZT and run the shared frost-zec checks plus sealForge's batch rules before signing (trust assumption 3): hidden outputs, foreign pools, inflated fees, a payout to anyone but the payout address, a protocol fee of the wrong amount, to the wrong address or paid twice, a consolidation that pays out, a non-standard refund amount, a refund note to any address but its bid's memo refund address, or a note paid twice are refused | `crates/sealforge-verify` (vendored frost-zec checks, `tests/tamper_vectors.rs`), `trustee::check_batch`, `sealforge-signer-wasm::verify_batch`. Mainnet gate: the second part |
| **Leaked admin token, or an operator raising a fee** | ⚠️ bounded | `SF_ADMIN_TOKEN` can only set an auction's fee rate, within 0 or 5–15%, and only before it opens. The rate is shown on the auction page from then on and published with the result; trustees pay exactly the published fee to the address fixed with it, once, and the auditor recomputes it. Organisers should check the rate before opening. Nothing else is reachable with the token | `routes/admin.rs`, `sealforge_core::fee::fee_rate_allowed`, migration `20261122010000` |
| **Coordinator operator, sealed-reveal mode** | ✅ prices hidden until the reveal window | During bidding it sees only deposits (a budget at or above the hidden price) and a commitment `BLAKE2b(code ‖ 0 ‖ refund_ua ‖ 0 ‖ price_le ‖ salt)`. Prices appear only in reveals mined after `close_height`. Deposit amounts are still visible to it | `sealforge_core::receipt::price_commitment`, `selection::classify_commit_reveal`, `tests/commit_reveal.rs`. Testnet `KE5DFF4PDA`, `K7GHHBC4GP` |
| **Single rogue trustee** | ✅ | FROST 2-of-3: one share can't produce a spend signature, and FROST refuses a one-signer package | `sealforge-escrow` tests `one_trustee_cannot_sign_alone`, `two_of_three_produces_consensus_valid_spend_auth_signatures`. `dkg_e2e` |
| **Coordinator during key generation** | ✅ | It only relays packages. Round 1 is signed by the sending trustee; round 2 is also sealed to the recipient's X25519 key. The escrow is derived only if all three trustees report the same public key package. Trustees compare a fingerprint out of band | `routes/dkg.rs`, `sealforge-cli::trustee`. `dkg_e2e::three_trustees_run_the_dkg_through_the_coordinator` |
| **Coordinator forcing FROST nonce reuse** (asking a trustee to sign different inputs over nonces it already used) | ✅ | A trustee records what it signed and deletes its nonces before sending. Retries resend the same bytes for identical inputs only; changed inputs wait for a fresh round | `sealforge-cli::trustee::sign_step`, browser `trustee-signing.tsx`. `dkg_e2e`. SECURITY-REVIEW F9 |
| **Coordinator equivocating between signers** (showing the two chosen signers different commitment sets, sighashes or randomizers) | ✅ detected before any share leaves | Both trustees commit before seeing each other's commitments, and the coordinator relays them, so it could show each signer a different set. FROST's binding factors keep that from forging a signature; what it gets is a signature that doesn't verify, from a signer who never learns why. Now each chosen signer signs a digest of its round 2 inputs with its trustee key (`sealforge-round2-inputs-v1`), and sends its share only once its co-signer's statement verifies, under the key on the public auction page, and matches its own. A mismatch is refused (class `equivocation`) and leaves two signed, contradictory statements as evidence | `sealforge_api::auth::round2_inputs_digest`; `sealforge-cli::trustee::check_co_signer_inputs`; browser `trustee-signing.tsx`; `dkg_e2e` |
| **Coordinator substituting the escrow address** (showing the address of a single-key wallet it controls instead of one derived from the FROST group key) | ✅ for trustees, ✅ for bidders with a residual | Each trustee unseals `nk ‖ rivk`, rebuilds the UA from its own group key, and refuses loudly on a mismatch; the coordinator won't open an auction until all three have signed an Ed25519 attestation over the UA. The auction page and the published result carry every trustee's key and attestation: the page checks them in the browser and blocks the bid form on a mismatch, and the auditor also checks that the published UIVK receives at that UA. Trustees run `trustee check` once the auction opens and `trustee sign` refuses if the public page shows another address or another key in their slot. Residual: a coordinator could invent all three trustees for bidders and show real trustees a different page; bidders catch that by checking each trustee's long-term key against the place it publishes it (see **Stand-in trustees** below), or by comparing per-auction keys the trustees gave them (`--trustee-key`) | `sealforge-cli::trustee::{verify_escrow, check_public}`, `routes/dkg.rs::verify_escrow`, `routes/auctions.rs::open`, `sealforge_auditor::check_escrow`, `sealforge_scan::escrow_address`. `dkg_e2e` (tampered `escrow_ua`, swapped trustee key), `escrow::tests`, `address::tests`. Reported privately 2026-11-17 |
| **Stand-in trustees** (a coordinator or organiser listing trustee keys of its own instead of the real trustees', or one party holding several seats) | ⚠️ partly: impersonation and key swaps ✅, independence ❌ | Each trustee can keep one long-term Ed25519 key, post it somewhere it controls (website, GitHub, X), and have it sign a certificate in every auction over the auction, slot, per-auction key and that place (`sealforge-trustee-cert-v1`). The page and the published result carry the certificates; the browser and `sealforge-audit` check them. A certificate that doesn't verify, or one long-term key on two seats, fails the escrow check and blocks bidding. A coordinator that swaps a certified trustee's key, or edits where it publishes, is caught with no pinning; stripping the certificate as well is caught by bidders who know that trustee's long-term key (`--trustee-identity`). **Residual:** keys prove who signed, not who the signers are. One party can make three long-term keys and three web pages, and a trustee with no long-term key could be anyone. Bidders have to recognise the places, and an organiser who picks all three trustees still picks all three. Independent trustee seats (the organiser chooses at most one) would close this; not built, as it needs a recruited trustee pool | `sealforge_api::auth::trustee_cert_message`; `sealforge_auditor::escrow` (`IdentityVerdict`, `require_identities`); `POST /v1/auctions/{code}/trustee-identity`; `dkg_e2e` |
| **Replay or forgery of organiser/trustee API calls** | ✅ | Ed25519 `SF-Sig` request signatures, ±60 s window, per-key nonce store | `sealforge-api::auth`, `api` integration tests |
| **Refund correlation at the exit** (amount matching when a bidder unshields or swaps a refund) | ✅ Mitigated | Canonical notes only, dust not sent, independent random delays, mixed batches. Measured: **0.1%** of refunds linkable vs **100%** for naive refunds | `sealforge-core::refunds`, `tools/linkability-harness`, [evidence](evidence/linkability.md) |
| **Source-chain observer (ShieldRamp)** | ⚠️ Not built | EVM deposits are public. Planned mitigations: canonical amounts, split swaps, delays, an ephemeral wallet and a Linkability Score shown before signing. Only the scoring module exists | `packages/shieldramp/src/score.ts` (Specified) |
| **The sealForge website** | ✅ | Receipt salts, refund addresses and receipts are created and kept in the browser. The audit runs the Rust auditor and scanner as WASM with published build provenance. Production CSP: same-origin scripts and WASM, `frame-ancestors 'none'`. No third-party font or analytics requests | `@sealforge/bid` (byte-compatible with Rust), `apps/web/public/*/BUILD.json`, SECURITY-REVIEW F5 |

## Data the coordinator holds

| Data | Protection | Retention |
|---|---|---|
| Escrow UFVK | `pgp_sym_encrypt` with `SF_DATA_KEY` | Until settled + 30 days |
| `nk`, `rivk` | Inside the UFVK above. Sampled by the coordinator, and sealed to each trustee so it can check the escrow address (trust assumption 2). Each trustee keeps the resulting FVK (`escrow-view.json`, owner-only) and puts it in its backup string | As the UFVK on the coordinator; trustees keep theirs |
| Refund addresses (from memos) | Encrypted column. Also readable by anyone with the published UIVK after publish | Until refunds confirm + 30 days |
| Organiser and trustee public keys | Public keys only | Life of the auction |
| FROST key shares | **Never held.** They stay on trustee machines (owner-only files) or browser memory. Each trustee's backup (`sealforge trustee backup`: key share plus escrow FVK, one bech32m string) is theirs to keep offline | n/a |
| Receipt salts | **Never held.** Browser only | n/a |
| Client IPs | Dropped from access logs (Caddy filter). Not logged by the app | n/a |

## Known limits

1. **Deposit mode exposes amounts to the coordinator operator and to every trustee.** Use sealed-reveal mode when that matters. Sealed-reveal still shows deposit sizes, which bound each price from above.
2. **The coordinator samples `nk`/`rivk`** and so can always view the escrow, and trustees get the same view so they can check the address. Who samples them doesn't affect spending: a UA rebuilt from the trustees' own `ak` can only be spent with the group key.
   - **Bidders get trustee keys from the coordinator.** The auction page checks every trustee's attestation over the exact address it shows, before the bid form continues, but the keys themselves are served by the coordinator. A coordinator that invents three trustees for bidders, or shows real trustees a different page, is caught only by bidders comparing keys with ones the trustees post themselves (`sealforge-audit --trustee-key`). Related: the organiser receives all three trustee invite codes to forward, so an organiser who keeps them can join as every trustee. Trustee independence rests on the trustees confirming, publicly, that they hold their slot.
   - **The UFVK is never published.** The UIVK published at close shows incoming notes only. Refunds and the payout are built with the escrow's outgoing viewing key (`OvkPolicy::Sender`), so publishing the UFVK, even at settlement, would let anyone read who was refunded what. Post-settlement disclosure of `nk ‖ rivk` alone would let an auditor rebuild the address from a group key, but trustees already do that before the auction opens.
3. **Trustee checks trust two inputs.** The published result's refund amounts and the trustee's chosen lightwallet server (trust assumption 3).
4. **Audit range.** The auditor scans the published block range by default. Bids outside it are not checked unless the auditor widens the range. Bidders' own receipt checks (`/verify`) catch omission of their own bid.
5. **Exit behaviour matters.** A bidder who exits all refund notes at once recreates their refund amount. The exit path must warn about this.
6. **Rare, large refunds** in small auctions can produce a uniquely sized note (the residual 0.1–0.4%, [evidence/linkability.md](evidence/linkability.md)).
7. **Unreadable memos and unusable refund addresses** can't be refunded automatically. They are kept for manual review (SECURITY-REVIEW F2).
8. **Mainnet has not been run.** Mainnet gates are listed in [SECURITY-REVIEW.md](SECURITY-REVIEW.md) §Open items and the docs site roadmap.
